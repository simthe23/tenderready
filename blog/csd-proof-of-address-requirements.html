CSD Proof of Address: What Counts, How Old It Can Be, and How It Gets You Disqualified

By Amanani (Pty) Ltd · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

Proof of address is one of the most boring documents on the CSD and one of the most common reasons a good quote gets rejected. A quote that is thousands of rand cheaper than everyone else's is worth nothing if the buyer checks your CSD record and finds an address document that is out of date.

Why buyers care

When you submit a quote, the procurement office usually checks your CSD supplier report before they compare prices. They are confirming that your business exists at the address you claim and that your details are current. If the document behind that address is older than their rule allows, they can mark you non-compliant and your price is never read.

Documents that are commonly accepted

What an entity accepts is set by that entity, and CSD guidance can change, so always confirm. Typical options are:

  • A recent utility bill (municipal rates, water, electricity) in the business name or the business address.
  • A signed lease agreement for the premises.
  • A bank statement or bank letter showing the business address.
  • For a home-based business, a letter from the property owner with a copy of their ID, along with a document showing the address.

The name and address on the document should match what is on the CSD and what is on your quote letterhead. If they differ, expect questions.

How old can it be?

A common rule of thumb is that proof of address should be no older than three months. The part that catches suppliers out is what the three months is measured against. Some RFQs measure the age from the closing date, not from today. A document that was fine when you uploaded it can be too old by the time the RFQ closes or by the time the buyer checks it.

Always read the RFQ conditions. If they state a window, follow the RFQ over any general rule.

A simple routine that prevents this

  1. Put a recurring reminder every two months to replace your proof of address and bank letter on the CSD, so they never reach the three-month mark.
  2. Before each quote, open your CSD supplier report and check the upload date of the address document against the closing date.
  3. Keep the original files in one folder with the date in the file name.
  4. Match the details on the document, the CSD, and your letterhead.

The bank letter has the same problem

The bank confirmation letter is checked the same way and has similar age expectations. A mismatch in the account holder name, or an old letter, can also delay payment after you win. Refresh both documents together.

What to do if it has already expired

Upload a new document right away, and keep proof of the upload date. If the RFQ has not yet closed, you may still be compliant on time. If it has closed, ask the buyer in writing whether they accept a late update. Do not assume they will. Some will not, and the answer is usually no.

An illustration of how it goes wrong

Here is a made-up example to show the mechanism. A supplier uploads a utility bill dated 2 June. In June it is fresh, and the CSD record looks fine. An RFQ is issued in August and closes on 20 September. When the buyer opens the CSD report on the closing date, the bill is about three and a half months old. If that buyer wants proof of address dated within three months of the closing date, the supplier is non-compliant, even though nothing about the business changed. The quote may never be priced against the others.

Home-based businesses and shared offices

Many small suppliers work from home or a shared space. The principle is that the address on the CSD should be where the business genuinely operates, and the document should connect the business, or its owner, to that address. If the utility bill is in the owner's name and not the company's, you may need an extra document such as a lease or a letter from the account holder. Rules differ between entities, so if you are unsure, ask the procurement office what they accept before you quote, not after.

Names and addresses must agree everywhere

A document can be recent and still fail if the details do not match. Check these three places against each other:

  • The name and address on the document you upload.
  • The address captured on your CSD record.
  • The address on your quote letterhead and returnable forms.

Spelling differences in the street name, a missing unit number, or an old postal address on the letterhead all give a reviewer a reason to query you.

Questions suppliers ask

Can I use a cell phone bill? Some entities accept it and some do not. A municipal account or lease is generally safer, but the RFQ and the entity's policy decide.

Does a photo of the document work? Only if every word is legible. An unreadable scan is treated as missing. A clean PDF is the safest format.

Do I have to upload it again for every RFQ? Usually no, because the CSD holds it. But you do have to make sure what is stored is still current on each closing date.

What if the buyer says my document is too old after the closing date? Ask them in writing whether a late replacement is allowed. Do not assume. In most cases the answer is no, which is why the check belongs before you submit.

One habit that fixes most of this

Keep a single folder for compliance documents, with the date in each file name, and a calendar entry two months out for each one. When a reminder fires, replace the document on the CSD that day. It costs you ten minutes every couple of months and removes a whole category of avoidable disqualification.
