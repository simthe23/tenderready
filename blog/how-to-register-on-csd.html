How to Register on the CSD (Central Supplier Database): A Step-by-Step Guide for South African SMMEs

By Amanani (Pty) Ltd · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

If you want to quote on South African government or parastatal RFQs, the Central Supplier Database (CSD) is where it starts. Almost every organ of state expects you to be registered, and many will not even open your quote without a valid CSD supplier number. This guide covers what the CSD is, what you need before you register, and the steps in order.

What the CSD is, and what it costs

The CSD is National Treasury's single database of suppliers to government. You register once, and departments, universities, municipalities and state entities can all look you up. Registration is free. Anyone asking you to pay to register on the CSD is not National Treasury, so treat it as a scam warning. You get a supplier number that starts with MAAA, and that number goes on every quote and invoice you send to the state.

The CSD does not just store what you type. It checks your details against other government records, including SARS, CIPC and Home Affairs, which is why the details have to match exactly.

What to have ready before you start

  • A valid email address and cell phone number. The phone receives a one-time PIN, so use a number you control.
  • Company registration details exactly as they appear on your CIPC documents: company name, registration number, and directors.
  • Director ID numbers.
  • Business bank account details in the company's name, plus a bank confirmation letter.
  • Tax details. Your SARS tax status feeds the CSD and drives your tax compliance status.
  • Proof of address for the business, such as a recent utility bill or lease.

Requirements can change, so confirm the current list on the official CSD website (www.csd.gov.za) before you begin.

The registration steps

  1. Create your user profile on the CSD website with your email, ID number and cell phone. Verify with the one-time PIN.
  2. Start a new supplier registration and select the supplier type, for example a private company.
  3. Enter your company details exactly as on your CIPC registration. A trailing "(Pty) Ltd" written differently is enough to cause a mismatch.
  4. Add your directors and ownership details.
  5. Load your banking details and upload the bank confirmation letter.
  6. Add your physical address and upload proof of address.
  7. Select the commodities or categories you supply, so buyers can find you.
  8. Submit, then wait for the verification results. Check your supplier summary report for the status of each item.

After you register: what keeps you eligible

Registering is the start, not the finish. Three things lapse quietly and cause disqualification later:

  • Tax compliance status. It has to be current on the day an RFQ closes, not just on the day you registered.
  • Proof of address and bank letter. Many entities want these to be recent, and some measure their age from the closing date.
  • Company details. If a director or address changes at CIPC, update the CSD too.

Download your CSD supplier summary report each time you quote and read it as the buyer would. If anything shows as not verified or not compliant, fix it before you submit.

Common mistakes that delay registration

  • Typing the company name differently from the CIPC certificate.
  • Bank account holder name not matching the company name.
  • Uploading an old or illegible proof of address.
  • Using a cell number or email you no longer check, then missing the verification messages.
  • Assuming registration means you are tax compliant. It does not; the two are separate.

What to do once you have your MAAA number

Put it on your quote letterhead, next to your company registration number and contact details. Then start screening RFQs. The next step is knowing which ones are worth your time, and what a compliant quote looks like. Our guide to what an RFQ is covers that.
