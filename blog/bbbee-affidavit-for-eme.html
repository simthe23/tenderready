B-BBEE Affidavit for an EME: Who Can Use One, What It Says, and Common Mistakes

By Amanani (Pty) Ltd · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

Almost every government RFQ asks about B-BBEE status. For small businesses, a full verification by an accredited agency can be expensive. The Codes of Good Practice let many small businesses prove their status with a sworn affidavit instead. This guide explains how that works. It is general information, not legal advice.

What an EME is

An Exempted Micro Enterprise (EME) is a business with annual turnover below the Codes' threshold, which is R10 million for most sectors. Some sector codes set different thresholds, so check the code that applies to your industry. Businesses above the EME threshold and up to R50 million fall under the Qualifying Small Enterprise (QSE) category.

The levels, and why ownership matters

Under the generic Codes, an EME gets an automatic B-BBEE level based on the share of black South African ownership:

  • 100% black ownership: Level 1
  • 51% to 99.99% black ownership: Level 2
  • Less than 51% black ownership: Level 4

Your level is not a choice. It follows the facts about who owns the business, so it must match your company's share register.

Affidavit or CIPC certificate

There are two common ways an EME shows its status:

  • A sworn affidavit. You complete the standard affidavit, sign it in front of a commissioner of oaths, and it is stamped. Once stamped, it can be used in place of a certificate.
  • A CIPC B-BBEE certificate. CIPC has an online function that issues a certificate to eligible EMEs. Eligibility conditions apply, and the platform has been updated recently, so check CIPC's current requirements.

Some buyers prefer one over the other, and some sector codes require a specific affidavit form. Read the RFQ to see which it accepts.

What the affidavit should cover

  • The business's details: name, registration number, and address.
  • The turnover for the latest completed financial year, not a future one.
  • The ownership details that justify your level.
  • One B-BBEE level only, marked in the box provided.
  • A commissioner of oaths' stamp and signature, and the deponent's signature.

How long it is valid

Affidavits are generally treated as valid for 12 months from the date signed, but buyers can set their own expectations. Diarise the date you signed it, and replace it before it expires. An old affidavit is one of the documents that quietly disqualifies a quote.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong turnover. Using this year's expected turnover instead of the latest completed financial year.
  • Wrong level. Claiming a level that does not match the ownership structure.
  • Unstamped affidavit. A signed but unstamped document is not a valid affidavit.
  • More than one level ticked.
  • Using the wrong template for a sector that has its own code.
  • Claiming status you cannot support. A false affidavit is a serious matter and can lead to disqualification and legal consequences. If you are unsure, leave the claim out or get advice.

What if you do not qualify for the affidavit?

If your turnover is above the limits for your category or your ownership does not support an affidavit, you need a verified B-BBEE certificate from an accredited agency. If you have none, you can still quote on many RFQs, but you will not earn any preference points that depend on B-BBEE status.

Before you submit

  1. Check the RFQ for what it accepts.
  2. Confirm the date on the affidavit or certificate is still valid on the closing date.
  3. Check the figures match your annual financial statements.
  4. Attach it with the other returnables.
